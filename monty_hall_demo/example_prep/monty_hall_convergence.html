Why does switching win more often?

When you first pick a door, you have a 1-in-3 chance of being right — meaning there is a 2-in-3 chance the prize is behind one of the other two doors. When the host opens a losing door, that 2/3 probability does not vanish. It collapses onto the one remaining closed door. Switching claims that 2/3 probability. Staying keeps your original 1/3.

The simulation cannot prove why — it shows you the shape of what happens at scale. Mathematics explains why. Code automates the drudgery of checking. Your curiosity decides how far to push it.
