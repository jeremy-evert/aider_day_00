# What SWOSU Copilot terms mean

Think in four layers:

1. **SWOSU.** The University controls the institutional account and use of
   University Information Systems under University policy. A student login is
   not a private personal sandbox.
2. **Microsoft.** Microsoft supplies the enterprise service under the
   customer organization's licensing agreement, Product Terms, Data
   Protection Addendum (DPA), service documentation, and tenant settings.
3. **Model provider.** Copilot may use Microsoft-operated models and/or
   third-party providers such as OpenAI or Anthropic, depending on the
   experience and configuration. A provider relationship is not automatically
   a direct consumer-account relationship.
4. **Student.** The student uses the service under access rules, course rules,
   platform terms, and applicable law and policy. The student must consider
   what is entered, what may be logged, what permissions apply, what may be
   redistributed, and what still needs independent verification.

Microsoft's current FAQ says organizational Microsoft 365 Copilot and Copilot
Chat are generally governed by the organization's Microsoft licensing
agreement together with applicable Product Terms, DPA, and SLAs. Microsoft
also documents Enterprise Data Protection (EDP) for users signed in with a
Microsoft Entra account. EDP is a contractual/data-handling commitment, not
invisibility: prompts and responses are not used to train foundation models,
while Microsoft 365/Purview capabilities can capture, audit, retain, or make
interactions available for eDiscovery according to organizational settings.

“Not used to train foundation models” does not mean “not logged.” Organization
permissions and retention policies still matter. A model name does not tell
you the whole data-governance story.

Before using it, ask your instructor or SWOSU ITS what experience and tenant
settings apply. Do not assume that a green shield, a model selector, Work IQ,
or a model name proves every contractual or privacy fact for this tenant.
